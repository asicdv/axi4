AW/W Channel Independence

Case 3 of 10 · Write data arrives before the address — and that is legal
—

0 Background read this first — context before mechanics

This case has no read-side equivalent. A read has one request channel; a write has two, and they are genuinely independent. Data may arrive before the address that tells the slave where to put it.

What independence actually means

AXI4 places no ordering requirement between the AW and W channels of the same transaction. All three of these are legal:

AW then W // the common case
AW and W // same cycle
W then AW // also legal — this case

More than that: AWVALID must not depend on WVALID, and WVALID must not depend on AWVALID. This is not merely permission to reorder — it is a prohibition on the master making one wait for the other. A master that holds WVALID low until its AW handshake completes is creating a dependency the protocol forbids, and in a system where the slave's AWREADY happens to depend on WVALID, that pair of assumptions deadlocks.

Why the protocol allows it

The two channels have different natural rates. An address is a few tens of bits and one handshake; a data burst can be 256 beats of 128 bytes. Forcing them into lockstep would mean the address channel idles for the duration of every burst, and a master could never run its address pipeline ahead of its data pipeline.

Running ahead is the point. A master that issues several addresses before their data lets the interconnect arbitrate, route and open DRAM rows while the data is still being assembled. By the time the beats arrive, the path is ready.

MasterWhy data may lead or lag the address
DMA engineReads its source data into a buffer, then writes. Data may be ready before the address channel wins arbitration

Cache writebackThe dirty line is already in the buffer; the address phase may queue behind other traffic

What the slave must therefore do

The slave's obligation

A slave must be able to accept W beats before it has seen the matching AW. That means buffering the data and associating it later. A slave whose WREADY is gated on having received an address is legal only if it never deadlocks — and proving that is harder than simply buffering.

The association rule is what makes it tractable: in AXI4 there is no WID, and write data must arrive in the same order as the addresses were accepted. So a slave that has buffered two bursts of data knows the first burst belongs to the first AW it receives, the second to the second, and so on. Order is the only association mechanism — which is precisely why AXI4 forbids interleaving write data from different transactions.

The deadlock this creates if you get it wrong

The classic failure is mutual waiting. Master: "I will not send W until AW is accepted." Slave: "I will not accept AW until I have W buffered." Neither is individually unreasonable; together they hang. This is why the specification states the independence as a requirement on the master rather than a permission — it removes one half of the deadlock by construction.
